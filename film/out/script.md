# Shell Asset Atlas: explainer script

5:35 in all. Written by `film/render.py` from `film/scenes.py`, with numbers from the data built 2026-11-11 (timeline to December 2025). Voice: Kokoro `af_heart` at speed 0.97. To replace a line with a real recording, save it as `film/out/vo/recorded/<id>.wav` and render again.

## 0:00 Under one platform

**Shows:** A cold open on the app's subsea view: the descent from the sea surface at Perdido, down the water column and in among the well paths. Then the title

**Camera and cursor:** No cursor. The app's own dive camera, opened from its deep link and slowed to three-quarter speed. The title fades up, the picture fades to black behind it, and the film cuts to a fresh load of the app: the globe arriving from space.

**Sound:** The pad fades in under the first line.

`0:00` `cold-1` This is the water under one platform in the US Gulf. 2,388 metres of it.

`0:07` `cold-2` And below the seabed: 80 wellbores, almost all of them drawn from survey records that anyone can download.

`0:15` `cold-3` This is the Shell Asset Atlas. An unofficial project, with no connection to Shell, built entirely from public data.

## 0:24 Public records, one globe

**Shows:** The turning globe and the headline figures in the side panel: production, installations, pipelines

**Camera and cursor:** The cursor appears and passes down the figures as each is named. The globe keeps turning.

**Sound:** Pad only.

`0:24` `atlas-1` Operators report what they produce to public regulators, every month. The atlas puts those records on a globe.

`0:31` `atlas-2` 210 installations and sites. 48 oil and gas fields. About 5,900 kilometres of pipeline. Drawn from regulators in the United States, the United Kingdom and Norway.

## 0:45 Every column is a hub

**Camera and cursor:** The cursor presses the US Gulf chip and the app flies in and tilts. It glides to the Mars tower, the hover card appears, and a click opens the details drawer.

**Sound:** A tick on each click.

`0:45` `gulf-1` Start in the US Gulf. Each column is a production hub, and its height is what that hub produced in a month.

`0:52` `gulf-2` Click one for its record. This is Mars: a tension-leg platform, standing in 894 metres of water since 1996, with every month of production since 2015.

## 1:06 A decade you can replay

**Shows:** The production timeline plays from the first month to the latest. The towers, the leaderboard and the storm tracks follow it

**Camera and cursor:** The drawer is closed to reveal the leaderboard. The cursor presses play and waits on the button while the decade runs, then moves to the total.

**Sound:** A tick on play.

`1:07` `time-1` Now press play. A decade of production replays, month by month, and the leaderboard re-ranks as it goes.

`1:14` `time-2` By December 2025, the total is about a million barrels of oil equivalent a day, gross. But look at the dips on the way. Several of them are storms.

## 1:27 What a hurricane does

**Shows:** The Gulf storms panel and the replay of Hurricane Ida: its track drawn across the Gulf, the towers falling in the month after, and the estimate of what was deferred

**Camera and cursor:** The cursor scrolls the side panel to the storm list and clicks Ida. The app jumps to the month before, flies to the Gulf and plays the storm through; the film shows that replay at two-fifths speed. The cursor then reads the hub-by-hub estimate and the weekly forecast snapshot.

**Sound:** Ticks on clicks.

`1:28` `storm-1` The atlas follows 26 Gulf storms that passed near Shell structures. The costliest was Hurricane Ida.

`1:35` `storm-2` Click it, and the atlas goes back to July 2021. Ida crosses the Gulf in August as a category four hurricane, and its centre passes two kilometres from the nearest Shell structure.

`1:47` `storm-3` The month after, Shell-operated production in the Gulf falls by more than half. Mars goes from about 182 thousand barrels of oil equivalent a day to nothing, and takes three months to recover.

`2:01` `storm-4` Across two hubs, the atlas estimates about 10 million barrels of oil equivalent deferred, relative to the pre-storm baseline. It's an estimate, and the panel says so.

`2:12` `storm-5` Each week, it also checks the National Hurricane Center's feed. At the last check, no storm was active.

**Sound:** Ticks on clicks and a soft tick on the key.

`2:19` `bench-1` The regulator publishes every operator, not only Shell. Press B, and the atlas ranks them all.

`2:25` `bench-2` Go back to 2015 and play the decade again. This time the bars are companies, ranked by the production they operate.

`2:39` `bench-3` Stop at September 2021, the month after Ida. BP has taken the lead.

`2:45` `bench-4` By December 2025, Shell ranks number one of 30 operators in the US Gulf, with about 28 percent of operated production. It has led in 123 of these 132 months.

`3:00` `bench-5` It isn't first everywhere. In Norway, Shell ranks number five, far behind Equinor. And this is production by operator of record, not equity.

## 3:09 Below the waterline

**Shows:** Search for Perdido, press Dive below, and the subsea view: the water column to scale, the surveyed well paths, the depth staff, the depth stretch, colour by spud year, a turn of the camera and a well's own record

**Camera and cursor:** The cursor switches to the seabed map, searches for Perdido and presses Dive below. It is hidden for the descent, which the film shows at three-fifths speed. The view then orbits by itself while the cursor reads the figures, presses Depth x3 and Spud year, drags the scene round and points at one well.

**Sound:** Ticks on clicks, soft ticks on keys. Nothing is added for the descent.

`3:10` `dive-1` Now, the place this film began. Search for Perdido: a spar, moored in 2,388 metres of water.

`3:18` `dive-2` Its record has one more button. Dive below.

`3:21` `dive-3` Everything here is drawn to true scale. The water is as deep as 2.9 Burj Khalifas, stood on end.

`3:28` `dive-4` Then the seabed, and below it, 80 wellbores. 76 of them follow the directional surveys filed with the regulator. The rest are drawn dashed, as schematics.

`3:39` `dive-5` The deepest ends 6,337 metres below sea level. Together, they come to about 157 kilometres of drilled hole.

`3:50` `dive-6` Stretch the depth three times, and each path is easier to follow.

`3:56` `dive-7` Colour them by the year drilling began, and the field grows in front of you, from 2002 to 2025.

`4:03` `dive-8` Drag to look around. Then point at any well for its name, its depth and its status.

`4:11` `dive-9` The atlas draws only what is public. The seabed is interpolated from well-site depths, and no geology is shown, because none is published.
